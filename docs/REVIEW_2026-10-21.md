# REVIEW 2026-10-21: free frame time, and the sea at coasts with poor data, at `aed9d06`

Mark, after the global atlas merged: "review the code again, same idea as the global alignment. I feel we
are missing out on some free frame times and possibly some cool simulations that we can do almost for
free that will make the coasts in places we might not have the nicest data for still have good and semi
accurate waves (well educated guesses at minimum not smoke and mirrors)."

The lens is today's: one global model, one law per measurement, no place standing in for another.

## How it was done, and what it can and cannot say

- **Measured**, not read off the code. Three cases, headless, 900 frames, `--gpu-time` (timestamps
  around every pass), one at a time on the one GPU (RTX 5060 Laptop):
  - **the Merrimack**: `scenes/merrimack.json`'s helm, the whole stack;
  - **orbit**: the NE pose of the rectangles bug, 546 km (`out/rect/ne546_on.json`);
  - **Cape Hatteras**: `world.json` with no place data (no solver, no wave-field window), the eye at
    400 m looking out to sea. This is the "coast without the nicest data".
- **Then one cost off at a time** to price each one. Shader costs used a scratch copy of `shaders/`
  through `--shaders` (the sky work's method); scene costs used `--set`. Each A/B is one 300-frame take.
  The floor between takes is about ±0.1–0.3 ms.
- **Blind spots.** Headless: no swapchain, no console, so the price of `Log` in a console window is not
  measured. One sun time (16:03 UTC). The water draw is timed on kept frames (the cached land, water
  redrawn), and the Merrimack keeps almost none, so its column below rests on a few frames each.

## Part A: free frame time

### What each cost is, switched off one at a time (the water draw, ms)

| switched off | Hatteras | orbit | Merrimack (kept frames only, p50) |
|---|---|---|---|
| nothing (base) | 17.31 | 5.71 | 9.53 (4 frames) |
| the terrain-shadow march (`render.shadowSteps 0`) | 14.71 (−2.67)\* | — | 8.54 (−1.0) |
| the sky along each reflected ray (`SkyRadianceDirDiscless`) | 15.24 (−2.07) | 4.96 (−0.76) | 9.13 (−0.4) |
| the air in front of the pixel (`SkyAir`, the view ray) | 15.65 (−1.66) | 5.00 (−0.71) | 9.25 (−0.3) |
| the cloud march (`CloudMarch`) | 15.96 (−1.35) | 4.71 (−1.00) | 9.34 (−0.2) |

| pixel water → vertex water (`water.pixelWater false`) | 12.23 (−5.16)\* | — | 10.75 (+1.2) |

At the helm the water is a small part of the frame: the camera rides the boat, so the kept frame almost
never holds (1 of 900 in the long run) and the 27.6 ms globe pass is mostly the whole surface redrawn.
A 300-frame take cannot split that pass while the scene is still streaming in (the takes' full-pass
means ran 19–30 ms), so the Merrimack column is the few kept frames' water alone, and noisy.

\* Against their own base take, 17.38 ms. None of these is a proposal to switch something off. They
price where the pixel's time goes.

### A1. The terrain shadow over open water: 2.7 ms at Hatteras; the better start is still open

`TerrainRay.hlsli` casts every pixel's sun ray over the height's maximum lane, skipping a block when the
ray clears its maximum. It starts at the finest block (the size of the first step) and climbs one level
per block. Out to `render.shadowRangeM` (8 km) over flat sea that is about 14 dependent lane reads per
pixel. Switching the cast off saves 2.67 ms at Hatteras.

**Tried, as a scratch shader:** start at the level of the whole range (the maximum-mipmap traversal from
the top, Tevs, Ihrke and Seidel, I3D 2008); the loop already descends where a bound fails. The frame is
identical (0 pixels differ at Hatteras). The time is a trade, not a win:

| | base | coarse-first |
|---|---|---|
| Hatteras, water draw | 17.32 ms | 16.41 ms (−0.91) |
| orbit, water draw | 5.71 ms | 5.69 ms |
| orbit, whole frame (more land) | 8.46 ms | 9.27 ms (+0.81) |

A coarse block that holds a dune fails the bound for a ray starting at sea level, so the traversal
descends to where it would have started anyway, and land pays for the climb down.

**Next to try:** a start level continuous in the sun's elevation. A high sun's ray rises out of a block
fast and can start coarse; a low sun's must start fine. Not measured.

### A2. The wave bank's rings filled where no pixel reads them: about 1.8 ms at altitude, exact

The bank fills 6 rings of 1.2–38 m texels under the eye (spans 0.6–20 km), then scatters the column
plane and its fold distance: 1.24 + 0.46 + 0.11 ms at the NE pose. There the nearest water is 526 km
away and a pixel's footprint is about 560 m. No pixel can read a 38 m texel, let alone a 1.2 m one, so
the work never reaches the picture (the sea past the rings reads the cascades).

**The law:** fill ring m only while a view's finest water footprint is within its texel's reach. That
is the fold doctrine the bank already applies to bands ("a rung carries a band only while it resolves
its phase"). Every eye counts, portal windows and the minimap included, and so does every hull's reader.
Mark's spinning-the-globe log shows the rings re-charting every frame at 7,300 km ("a new cell"), so
the fill also restarts every frame there.

### A3. The air's marches per water pixel: about 3.7 ms at Hatteras, 1.5 ms at orbit (his call)

Each water pixel marches the air three times, 10 exponential steps each (`AtmRay`): along the view ray
from the eye, and along its reflected ray from the water (`SkyRadianceDirDiscless`, for the mirror and
again for the light behind a crest).

The reflected one starts at the sea's surface under the water's own zenith. For a spherically
symmetric air that depends only on three angles: the ray's zenith, the sun's zenith, and the angle
between them. So it is a function of the air, not of any eye. That is Bruneton and Neyret's (2008) 4-D
scattering table at r = R, a 3-D table (for example 64 × 32 × 32), built when the sun moves. It would
be right from every eye and through every portal, which the per-eye sky-view table Mark rejected on
2026-09-16 was not (it showed the Merrimack's sunset at Haulover).

It is still a table, and he asked then for every ray to march the air, so this is his decision. Built
with 40 steps it would also be more accurate than today's 10-step march. The view ray's air has no such
symmetry (it starts at the eye), so it stays a march.

### A4. The cloud march over clear air: 1.0–1.35 ms, exact fix

`CloudMarch` takes 14 steps through the cloud shell for every surface pixel and reads the cloud bank,
where NULL tiles read zero. Over clear air it marches zeros: 1.35 ms at Hatteras and 1.00 ms at orbit.
An empty-space skip on the bank's maximum (one coarse read: is there any cloud along this ray's span?)
gives the same picture. It is the maximum-lane idea of A1, applied to the cloud bank.

### A5. The walk's bookkeeping at the helm: 14.66 ms of CPU per frame (measure the fix first)

At the Merrimack the camera rides the boat, so the eye moves every frame and the globe walk re-runs
every frame (822 of 900). The walk itself is 0.11 ms. The rest is:

- **8.91 ms emitting residency wants:** 85,623 `Want()` calls a frame for 4,740 leaves. Leaves share
  tiles: 14,219 cube and 36,215 window wants over 23,698 (world, slice) pairs. The column scan reads
  178,792 stamps and the weight loop visits 446,497 slots.
- **5.64 ms building meshlet records.**

The GPU waits on the CPU 5.15 ms a frame there (`gpu.gap`), so this is frame time, not hidden work.
Asking once per distinct tile rather than once per leaf corner should cut the 85,623. I have not
measured that, so it is first a counter: how many distinct tiles do the 85,623 calls name?

### A6. `Log` on the frame thread (not measured: the headless runs have no console)

`Log` formats, then under one global mutex calls `fputs`, `fflush(stdout)` and `OutputDebugStringA`
twice, on the calling thread. Mark's log of the spin shows about ten event lines a frame from the frame
thread ("the rings' chart is the cloud's origin … (a new cell)", five "sun map k" lines, "frame N: eye
at"). In a console window each line is a console write. The cheap fix keeps every gate line and moves
lines that fire every frame under motion behind a trace switch, or counts them the way "statements
repeated by number" already does. Measure first in the interactive build: frame time spinning the
globe with the lines on and off.

### Not proposed: pixel water (5.16 ms at Hatteras)

Pixel water is the look Mark chose over vertex water. The number is here so the budget is honest.
It is not cheaper everywhere: at the helm the vertex-shaded sea took 10.75 ms of kept-frame water against
about 9.5 ms, its cost moved into the mesh stage.

## Part B: the sea at a coast without good data

### What Cape Hatteras gets today (traced, and confirmed in its log)

- **The spectrum is another place's.** The synthesis takes one sea state, the forecast point nearest
  the scene's place (`Assembly.cpp`). The world composite holds six points: three off the Merrimack, two
  off Haulover, one off Mavericks. Hatteras synthesized `gfswave:42.5,-70.75`, the Gulf of Maine
  (reference Hs 0.625 m), so its swell periods and directions are the Gulf of Maine's.
- **Only the height is local.** `WaveScale` multiplies the bands by the global gfswave Hs grid (0.25°)
  over the reference. That grid is from cycle `20260828t18z`, nearly two months old.
- **At the coast the height is not local either.** gfswave masks land and its coastal nodes, and
  "absence reads the reference": scale 1, so the Gulf of Maine's sea arrives on the North Carolina
  shore unchanged.
- **The swell shadow** marches toward that same borrowed peak direction.
- **What is already global and right:** the bank's per-texel depth physics on every coast the rings
  reach. That is shoaling (Green's law), the wave-current gain, depth-limited breaking (the clipped
  Rayleigh sea), the depth's orbit (coth kh), the crest law and the lip's fall. The bed is the global
  atlas.
- **What exists only in a place's window:** refraction. `WaveField`'s solve does shoaling × refraction
  × limiter with integrated phase per spectral component, but only inside the place's `wavefield`
  window, and its Snell's law is against one hand-set `barNormalDeg` (the Merrimack's bar, 285°).
- **Where the forecast has no wind sea**, it is filled with Pierson-Moskowitz, which the code calls
  "the fetch-UNLIMITED answer", capped at the forecast's own Hs.

So at a coast with no place, the sea is a distant place's sea, scaled by a stale height, transformed
correctly by depth, and never turned toward the beach.

### The proposals, cheapest first. Each is a published law on data the engine already has or can fetch.

**B1. Every place's own spectrum: data, no new law.** The point harvest already reads every partition
from the GFS-Wave files (`Waves.cs`: wind sea `WVHGT`/`WVPER`/`WVDIR`, each swell train's
`SWELL`/`SWPER`/`SWDIR`, and `PERPW`/`DIRPW`). The global grid's harvest asks NOMADS for only
`HTSGW` and `WIND` (`Globe.cs`). Adding the partitions to that one request, and refreshing it each
cycle (the loaded grid is from `20260828t18z`), makes the spectrum's period and direction a global
field like the Hs ratio is. The synthesis's one spectrum then needs a local
reshape. The texel already carries per-band gains (`gainC`), and the lattice charts already rotate the
bands' vector channels into each place's axes (`ChartRot`). A per-chart rotation by the local mean
direction, plus band gains from the local spectrum's shape, is the variance-preserving blend the charts
were built for.

**B2. Absence reads the nearest sea, not the reference.** Where gfswave masks a coastal node, read the
nearest wet node's sea state (deep-water values) and let the bank's shoaling and breaking carry it in.
That is the standard nearest-wet-node fill, and it replaces "the Gulf of Maine's sea at every coast".
One law, continuous across the coast.

**B3. Refraction on any coast, with the solve the engine already has.** Run `WaveField` as a tile node
over the coast near the eye. Cache it on disk per sea-state bucket, as the Merrimack's solve is (it
prefilled 7,359 tiles in 2.05 s on a worker, the frame not waiting). Two changes make it global:
- the spectrum is the place's own (B1);
- Snell's law is against the contour normal of the bed's own gradient at the wave's scale, in place of
  `barNormalDeg`. That is the textbook local straight-contour approximation, one law, no hand number.

It costs background CPU, not frame time, and the bank already blends a solved field over the cascades.
This is what makes waves wrap and line up with a beach.

**B4. A wind sea grown by its fetch.** Replace the fetch-unlimited Pierson-Moskowitz fill with
fetch-limited growth (JONSWAP; Carter 1982: Hs ≈ 0.0163 X^0.5 U10, Tp ≈ 0.566 X^0.3 U10^0.4, X in km;
CEM Part II-2). The fetch is the distance to land upwind, which the exposure's line-of-sight march
already measures, toward the wind instead of toward the swell. The 10 m wind grid is already loaded.
Bays, sounds, estuaries and lakes, where gfswave has no data at all, get the wind sea their own
geometry allows, rather than none or a stranger's.

**B5. The water's edge moves with the waves: setup and runup (Stockdon et al. 2006).** A beach's mean
waterline rises with wave setup and swings with swash. Stockdon's empirical fit (10 field experiments,
rms error 38 cm on a mean runup of 1.44 m) needs:
- the offshore height and wavelength: B1's H0, and L0 from Tp;
- the foreshore slope, from the bed.

The water bank's level plane already holds the departure from the waterline, so setup is one more term
in it, and swash its slow oscillation (the infragravity band). Of the five, this is the one a person
standing on the beach would notice first.

**Later, after B3: a longshore current** from the breaking angle (Longuet-Higgins 1970) to drift the
foam along the beach.

## What I'd do first

1. **A2 and A4:** exact by construction (no pixel can read what is skipped). The prices are about
   1.8 ms at altitude for A2 and 1.0–1.35 ms over clear air for A4. Each would be built and measured
   before it counts.
2. **B1 and B2:** data plus one law; every coast stops borrowing the Gulf of Maine.
3. **B4, then B5:** cheap, visible, and each a published law.
4. **B3:** the larger piece. The solver exists; it moves from a window to tiles.
5. **A1's start rule:** one more shader variant.
6. **A3:** Mark's call (a table of the air).
7. **A5 and A6:** a counter and an interactive measurement before any change.
